Conduit tools · First working module

Conduit Bending

Calculate common EMT bend marks. Confirm results against your bender's markings and the actual conduit before making a production bend.

Size selection is recorded for your workflow. Bend geometry and take-up can vary by bender and shoe; enter the manufacturer's take-up for stub-ups.

Enter the take-up printed on your bender or provided by its manufacturer. It varies by bender and conduit size.

This estimates the distance from the obstruction/contact point to the bend mark using rise ÷ sin(angle). Actual mark placement depends on how you measure the kick and the bender's geometry.

Enter the obstacle's rise and width. The calculator lays out a center mark and two symmetric outer marks using a simplified geometric model. Verify the method against a trusted field reference and your bender before using the marks on an installation.

Field check: These calculations provide starting measurements, not a guarantee of bend accuracy. Account for bender-specific geometry, conduit springback, spacing, and job conditions. Measure the actual result before cutting or installing.

Offset angle guide

Larger bend angles shorten mark spacing but generally create more shrink and can be harder to pull through crowded runs. Choose an angle that suits the obstruction and the available space.

Visual bend guides

Choose a guide to see the reference marks and bending sequence. Diagrams are schematic and not to scale.

Sequence: Mark the calculated spacing, bend the first angle, then rotate the conduit 180° and make the second bend to bring the run back parallel.

Sequence: Use the take-up for your actual bender, mark from the conduit end, align the mark with the bender's reference, and bend to 90° while checking the finished height.

Diagrams illustrate the general idea only. Follow your bender's instructions for hook, arrow, star, or notch alignment; reference points differ between tools.

Saddle bend diagrams

Schematic layouts for understanding the bend order. Mark spacing and angles depend on your chosen method; use the calculator results as preliminary estimates.

General sequence: Mark the obstacle center and the two outer bend locations using your verified layout. Make the center bend, then the two outer bends in the appropriate opposing direction for your method. Check alignment and clearance before finalizing.

Field caution: These illustrations show the concept, not exact bend geometry or clearance. Confirm angle convention, mark spacing, shrink, and sequence against a trusted conduit-bending reference and your bender instructions before installing.

Enter any two values to solve for voltage, current, resistance, and DC power where mathematically possible.

For DC resistive circuits. AC calculations involving impedance, power factor, harmonics, or non-linear loads require additional information.

Electrical calculations

Voltage Drop

Estimate circuit voltage drop using conductor material, size, length, current, and circuit type. This is a planning estimate, not final conductor sizing.

Model assumptions: simplified K-method using one-way length and a circuit factor of 2 for single-phase/DC two-wire circuits or √3 for three-phase. Actual AC impedance, conductor temperature, terminals, load characteristics, and applicable code requirements can change the result. Verify with approved tables and project conditions.

Quick References

Core formulas

Ohm's law
V = I × R   ·   I = V ÷ R   ·   R = V ÷ I

DC power
P = V × I   ·   P = I²R   ·   P = V² ÷ R

Single-phase real power
P = V × I × power factor

Balanced three-phase real power
P = √3 × line voltage × line current × power factor

AC power formulas require the appropriate RMS values and assumptions. Use the correct formula for the circuit and load.
